**Applies to:** MAELVYN brand, venture studio, Agentic OS, ventures, products, commerce and distribution

> **Building Businesses. Creating Assets. Growing Together.**  
> **Participation by Choice. Ownership by Agreement.**

## 1. Executive definition

**MAELVYN is an Agentic Shared Venture Studio:** a real business-building and operating ecosystem that uses governed human decision-making, specialist AI agents, software, provider integrations and shared operating capabilities to discover, validate, build, launch and operate independently accountable ventures and productive assets.

MAELVYN is not a hobby project, a collection of disconnected demos, an AI chatbot wrapper, a freelancer directory, a public investment marketplace, or merely a SaaS dashboard.

The system has two connected but distinct identities:

1. **MAELVYN — the venture studio and business ecosystem.** It establishes strategy, creates and operates ventures, builds assets, and coordinates shared capabilities.
2. **MAELVYN OS — the agentic operating system.** It is the governed software and automation infrastructure used to operate the studio and its ventures.

The OS serves the business. The business is not justified merely by the existence of the OS.

## 2. Category and positioning

### Category
Agentic Shared Venture Studio / Venture-Building Operating Ecosystem.

### Positioning statement
For founders, operators and venture teams who need to turn opportunities into operating businesses, MAELVYN combines venture-building discipline with a governed agentic operating system. Unlike a generic AI assistant, agency, marketplace or passive holding structure, MAELVYN connects evidence, accountable people, specialist agents, production capabilities, commercial execution and venture-level economics in one controlled operating model.

### Short description
A venture studio that builds and operates businesses and productive assets with human accountability and agentic systems.

### Brand promise
Turn validated opportunities into real products, revenue-generating operations and durable assets—without obscuring ownership, responsibility, costs or risk.

### Message hierarchy
- **Primary:** Building Businesses. Creating Assets. Growing Together.
- **Supporting:** Participation by Choice. Ownership by Agreement.
- **Product:** MAELVYN OS — Governed Agentic Operations for Venture Building.
- **Proof:** validated demand, shipped products, paid customers, reliable delivery, reconciled economics and evidence-backed venture decisions.

### What MAELVYN must never claim without proof
- Guaranteed income, returns, growth or investment outcomes.
- Existing partnerships, investors, shareholders or participant commitments that have not been formally agreed.
- Fully autonomous business operation where human approvals remain necessary.
- Official platform publishing or seller API integration where only public-data extraction is available.
- Ownership of a venture, asset, domain, brand or IP without documented rights.

## 4. Business model: what MAELVYN actually does

MAELVYN operates through four business motions.

### A. Venture creation
- Discover problems and market opportunities.
- Research customers, alternatives, willingness to pay and economics.
- Validate with interviews, landing pages, prototypes, pre-orders or paid pilots as appropriate.
- Approve a venture charter with accountable operator, scope, budget, risks and stop criteria.
- Build and launch a product or service.
- Operate, measure, improve, scale, hold, restructure or close.

### B. Venture operations
Provide shared capabilities to approved ventures: research, product development, software, automation, creative production, finance operations, analytics, customer operations and growth. Any chargeback, service fee, license or resource allocation must be documented per venture.

### C. Product and asset commercialization
Create or acquire rights to products, software, services, content, IP and productive assets; package them into clear offers; sell, license, subscribe, distribute or otherwise commercialize them through eligible channels.

### D. Portfolio coordination
Maintain venture-level governance, budgets, ownership and IP records, risk registers, operating evidence, asset records, performance and portfolio decisions. Consolidated reporting must preserve venture-level truth.

## 5. Revenue and monetization architecture

Revenue is earned by the legal seller/operator or rights-holder identified for each transaction. MAELVYN must not blur studio revenue, venture revenue, customer funds and participant capital.

### Monetization streams

| Stream | What is sold | Revenue model | Activation condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Venture-owned products | Physical or digital products | One-time sale / bundles | Valid offer, seller rights, fulfillment and margin |
| Software and digital tools | Software, SaaS, templates, systems, licenses | Subscription / license / one-time | Working product, support and renewal terms |
| Productized services | Research, software, automation, creative and business services | Fixed scope / package / milestone | Delivery specification, capacity, QA and terms |
| Managed venture services | Shared operating capabilities | Retainer / service fee / approved allocation | Written inter-venture service agreement and cost allocation |
| Distribution and partnerships | Qualified referrals, channel access, reseller services | Disclosed commission / margin / partner fee | Channel terms, attribution and legal eligibility |
| IP and asset licensing | Software, content, methods, brands or other rights | License fee / royalty | Verified chain of title and written license |
| Venture participation | Agreed ownership or performance participation | Contractual distributions / exit proceeds | Separate legal, tax, valuation and rights-holder approval |

### Monetization order for initial execution
1. Validate and sell one clearly scoped product or service to a real customer.
2. Measure delivery cost, gross margin, support burden, refunds and repeat demand.
3. Standardize successful delivery into a repeatable offer and SKU.
4. Add recurring subscriptions or licensing only where ongoing value and retention justify them.
5. Add channel/partner commissions where the channel has verifiable attribution and compliant terms.
6. Consider venture equity or other participation only through a separate written and legally reviewed agreement.

Do not launch a public investment product, pooled wallet, profit-sharing promise, or open multi-vendor marketplace as an MVP.

### Unit economics required per offer
- Price and currency.
- Seller/legal counterparty and tax treatment.
- Direct provider, production and fulfillment costs.
- Payment and channel fees.
- Refund, revision, support and warranty allowance.
- Contribution margin and minimum acceptable margin.
- Delivery capacity and time.
- Rights, permitted use, exclusions and expiry.
- Customer acquisition source and attributable acquisition cost.

A price is not approved merely because it is competitive. An offer must be deliverable, rights-cleared and economically viable.

## 6. Customer and market model

MAELVYN has several distinct audiences. They must not be collapsed into one generic user role.

1. **Commercial customers:** buy a product, license or service from the correct venture/seller.
2. **Venture operators:** own day-to-day execution and are accountable for venture outcomes.
3. **Contributors and specialists:** perform a defined role under explicit scope and rights.
4. **Partners and channels:** refer, resell, distribute or provide infrastructure under their own terms.
5. **Approvers and rights-holders:** make decisions reserved to them by governance and agreement.
6. **Studio stewards:** maintain shared strategy, policies, controls and portfolio reporting.

Participation in the studio is not automatically an offer to invest, a promise of equity, employment, partnership or entitlement to venture profits.

## 7. Commercial customer journey

`Discover → Understand Offer → Verify Seller & Terms → Request / Configure → Quote → Order → Pay (if enabled) → Fulfill → QA → Deliver → Accept / Revise → Support → Repeat`

Required customer-visible capabilities:
- Public offer and seller information.
- Clear scope, exclusions, price or quote method, delivery estimate and rights.
- Brief or configuration intake.
- Order confirmation and customer-safe progress.
- Payment status only when connected to a verified payment provider.
- Secure delivery and acceptance.
- Revision, refund and support policies.
- Privacy notice and terms appropriate to the actual seller and service.

Customer-visible status is a safe projection of internal workflow. It is never a raw agent or provider execution log.

## 8. Venture operating workflow

`Signal → Opportunity → Research → Validation → Venture Charter → Approval → Build → QA → Launch → Operate → Review → Scale / Hold / Restructure / Exit`

Each gate records:
- accountable human owner;
- evidence and source provenance;
- assumptions and confidence;
- budget, costs and resource commitments;
- risks, legal and rights checks;
- decision and authorized approvers;
- success metric, stop condition and next review date.

An AI-generated report, prototype, waitlist or social engagement is not by itself proof of a viable business. Paid customer behavior, repeat usage, delivery quality and unit economics are stronger evidence.

## 9. External and internal trust boundaries

### External zone
- Customer Experience.
- Commerce and catalog.
- Distribution and Market Access.

### Internal zone
- Shared Economic Core and governance.
- MAELVYN Agentic OS and orchestration.
- Product, service and venture factories.
- Provider and integration fabric.
- Internal finance, audit, rights and risk controls.

The only permitted path is:

`Customer / Commerce / Distribution`
→ `authenticated application contract`
→ `OS command/event boundary`
→ `policy, approval and budget gate`
→ `factory task contract`
→ `provider fabric`
→ `factory output and QA`
→ `OS approval`
→ `approved commerce or distribution action`
→ `customer-safe projection`

Prohibited: direct customer-to-factory, commerce-to-factory, distribution-to-factory, factory-to-provider, or browser-to-provider execution.

See `docs/EXTERNAL-INTERNAL-BOUNDARY-CONTRACT.md` and `docs/PLATFORM-LAYERED-ARCHITECTURE.md`.

## 10. Factory portfolio

Factories are internal production capabilities, not separate public customer applications by default.

| Factory | Core outputs | Example commercial form |
|---|---|---|
| Research & Intelligence | Market evidence, customer insight, competitor and opportunity briefs | Research package / internal venture decision |
| Software & Product | Websites, applications, APIs, SaaS and integrations | Project fee / license / subscription |
| Automation & Operations | Workflows, integrations, agentic processes and operational systems | Setup fee / managed service / license |
| Creative & Media | Design, video, content, campaigns and media assets | Fixed package / retainer / licensing |
| Digital Product | Templates, guides, systems, educational and downloadable assets | One-time sale / bundle / subscription |
| Business & Service Operations | Defined operational and specialist services | Package / retainer / managed service |
| Commerce Operations | Catalog, pricing, order, fulfillment and channel operations | Venture service fee / approved transaction economics |

Every factory needs a versioned deliverable specification, input contract, output schema, QA rubric, rights checklist, pricing model, cost model, service-level expectation and failure/revision path.

## 11. Distribution and market access

Distribution is a channel layer, not the production system.

Potential channel classes:
- MAELVYN and venture-owned websites.
- Indonesian commerce marketplaces.
- Global digital-product marketplaces.
- Freelance/service marketplaces.
- Social content and social commerce.
- Affiliate, reseller and referral partners.
- Direct B2B sales and strategic partnerships.

Each channel is classified independently:
- **Research-only:** public discovery and competitor intelligence.
- **Assisted/manual:** human creates or updates the listing.
- **Draft integration:** API can prepare a draft but not publish.
- **Write-enabled:** verified official or authorized seller-side API with required account scopes.
- **Live:** approved write integration, tested idempotency, rollback/recovery and audit.

Never infer seller-side publishing capability from public product search. Never automate a channel using scraping where its terms or access controls do not permit it. Channel-specific account eligibility, fees, payout, seller identity, API scopes and legal terms must be verified before launch.

## 12. Brand and communication rules

- Speak as a disciplined venture studio, not as a speculative investment club.
- Lead with outcomes and proof, not agent counts or technical novelty.
- Show real products, delivery evidence, customer outcomes and transparent commercial terms.
- Distinguish MAELVYN from each venture's independent brand and seller identity.
- Never expose provider names, internal prompts, secret routing, private customer data or internal margin in customer-facing materials.
- Do not state that a product is AI-generated or human-made inaccurately; disclose material facts where required by law, platform policy or customer terms.
- Obtain permission and rights clearance before using customer work, third-party media, testimonials, logos or case studies.

## 13. Business performance system

### North Star
**Evidence-Backed Active Ventures (EBAV):** ventures in Build, Launch or Operate with a named accountable operator, current approved charter and budget, recent evidence, and no unresolved critical governance or safety gate.

### Commercial metrics
- Paid orders and successful fulfillment.
- Net revenue by legal seller, venture, product and channel.
- Contribution margin and gross margin.
- Customer acquisition cost and payback.
- Conversion, refund, dispute and repeat-purchase rates.
- Delivery time, QA pass rate and revision rate.
- Cash runway and budget variance.
- Asset utilization and return where applicable.

### Agentic operations metrics
- Verified task completion rate.
- Human rework rate.
- Cost per accepted outcome.
- Policy and approval integrity.
- Recovery without duplicate side effects.
- Evidence coverage and source quality.
- Cross-venture isolation violations (target zero).

Agent count, generated content volume, impressions, catalog size and GMV are not success measures on their own.

## 14. Implementation architecture

The platform is implemented as bounded domains with explicit contracts, not as a monolithic dashboard.

1. **Identity & Access:** separate customer identity, internal membership, roles, venture scopes and service identities.
2. **Customer Experience:** public site, customer workspace, intake, order view, delivery and support.
3. **Commerce Domain:** catalog, offers, product versions, SKUs, quotes, orders, entitlements, payment references and refunds.
4. **Agentic OS:** task contracts, orchestration, agent registry, workflow runtime, policy, approvals, evaluations, event handling and status projections.
5. **Factory Runtime:** factory registry, execution workers, deliverable schemas, QA, artifact lifecycle and retry/cancel behavior.
6. **Provider Fabric:** adapter registry, capabilities, credentials, quotas, pricing, rights, budget gates, health and usage ledger.
7. **Distribution Domain:** channel registry, export packages, listing drafts, publication approvals, channel IDs, sync status and analytics.
8. **Shared Economic Core:** venture governance, finance, budgets, ownership, contributions, assets, audit and portfolio reporting.
9. **Control Plane:** internal administration, security, policies, monitoring, operational configuration and incident controls.

### Platform rules
- Cloudflare-first deployment with isolated development and production environments.
- D1 for transactional application data; private R2 for protected artifacts; Queues/Workflows only where durable asynchronous processing is required.
- Hono server-side APIs and explicit DTOs; React/TypeScript user interfaces.
- Clerk identity integration with server-side authorization and role/venture scoping.
- No secret in browser bundles, D1 plaintext, public repository or chat.
- All external side effects use idempotency, bounded retries and audit records.
- Paid provider calls remain disabled until provider price, rights, budget cap and approval behavior are verified.
- No production schema mutation or deployment as a side effect of documentation work.

## 15. Build and commercial launch sequence

### Phase 0 — Business truth and legal/operating readiness
Confirm brand claims, actual operator/seller, legal status, applicable KBLI/permissions, tax, privacy, IP rights, operating responsibilities and venture-level separation.

### Phase 1 — One controlled commercial vertical slice
Choose one validated customer segment and one offer. Deliver:
- offer and seller record;
- customer brief/intake;
- quote/order record;
- OS task and approval;
- factory production;
- QA and private delivery;
- customer acceptance/support;
- actual cost, revenue and margin reporting.

Use human-assisted fulfillment where integrations are not verified.

### Phase 2 — Repeatability
Turn the successful delivery into versioned product specifications, packages, SKUs, pricing, SOPs, QA tests and reusable workflows.

### Phase 3 — Distribution
Prepare channel-specific listing packages, test seller eligibility and API scopes, then activate only approved channels. Draft-first before any live publishing.

### Phase 4 — Portfolio scale
Add additional factories, ventures, subscriptions, partners and consolidated reporting only after the first commercial unit demonstrates repeatable demand and positive contribution economics.

## 16. Non-negotiable launch gates

A feature or venture is not production-ready until applicable checks pass:
- role and workspace authorization tests;
- cross-venture isolation tests;
- privacy and security review;
- legal seller and rights verification;
- pricing and unit economics;
- QA and fulfillment acceptance;
- idempotency and failure recovery;
- audit and reconciliation;
- backups and restore verification;
- support and refund path;
- human approval of material commitments.

## 17. Decision record and unresolved items

This charter resolves the strategic category and operating model. It does not fabricate market validation or legal facts.

The following require evidence before final commercial activation:
- first customer segment and validated problem;
- first product/service offer and tested willingness to pay;
- actual seller/operator for each offer;
- current legal status, KBLI, permits and tax treatment;
- final brand assets and trademark clearance;
- provider costs and commercial rights;
- channel seller eligibility and write scopes;
- payment, settlement, refund and fulfillment provider readiness.

These are evidence gates, not reasons to turn MAELVYN into a hobby or stop implementation. Build the operating system in parallel, while keeping live commercial commitments gated until verified.
