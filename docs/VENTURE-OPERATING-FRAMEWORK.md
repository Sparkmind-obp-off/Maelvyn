# MAELVYN — Venture Creation & Portfolio Operating Framework

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Proposed operating framework

## 1. Objective

Provide one repeatable process for moving a business opportunity from an initial signal to a measured operating venture, while controlling cost, risk, ownership, and decision-making.

## 2. Venture lifecycle

`Discover → Validate → Build → Launch → Operate → Scale / Hold / Exit`

### Stage 0 — Discover

**Purpose:** Create a qualified opportunity, not a premature commitment.

Activities:
- Identify a customer segment and recurring problem.
- Research existing alternatives and competitors.
- Estimate market access and distribution difficulty.
- Identify relevant capabilities and dependencies.
- Record the source and confidence of each important claim.

Gate to proceed:
- A clearly described customer problem.
- A plausible target customer.
- A testable hypothesis.
- No obvious disqualifying legal or ethical issue.

### Stage 1 — Validate

**Purpose:** Test whether a real customer need and viable commercial path exist.

Activities:
- Conduct ethical customer discovery.
- Test a landing page, prototype, offer, or manual service.
- Seek behavioral evidence such as paid pilots, deposits where lawful, repeat use, or qualified purchase intent.
- Estimate pricing, costs, gross margin, and acquisition effort.
- Record negative evidence and unresolved assumptions.

Gate to proceed:
- Evidence of demand proportionate to the proposed investment.
- A credible initial unit-economics model.
- A defined pilot budget and stop condition.

### Stage 2 — Build

**Purpose:** Create the smallest reliable solution that can deliver the promised outcome.

Activities:
- Define scope and acceptance criteria.
- Assign an accountable owner.
- Build the MVP or operational prototype.
- Document suppliers, licenses, data flows, and dependencies.
- Test quality, security, and customer experience.

Gate to proceed:
- MVP meets acceptance criteria.
- Critical risks have owners and mitigations.
- Launch costs and operating responsibilities are understood.

### Stage 3 — Launch

**Purpose:** Introduce the offer to a controlled customer group.

Activities:
- Publish accurate positioning, pricing, terms, and support expectations.
- Set up sales, delivery, payment, and complaint handling.
- Track acquisition, conversion, fulfillment, refunds, and customer feedback.
- Keep commitments within approved scope and capacity.

Gate to proceed:
- Customers can buy and receive the offer reliably.
- Financial and operational records are working.
- No unresolved critical launch issue remains.

### Stage 4 — Operate

**Purpose:** Establish repeatable delivery and positive operating discipline.

Activities:
- Monitor revenue, direct costs, margin, cash, and liabilities.
- Track customer retention, complaints, and service quality.
- Improve processes and remove recurring failure points.
- Review budget against actuals.
- Maintain asset, contract, and IP records.

Gate to proceed:
- Reliable delivery.
- Understandable unit economics.
- Evidence of repeat demand.
- Risks remain within approved tolerance.

### Stage 5 — Scale, Hold, Restructure, or Exit

Choose based on evidence, not momentum.

- **Scale:** Increase investment only when demand, economics, capacity, and controls support it.
- **Hold:** Preserve a sound venture while gathering more evidence or waiting for conditions.
- **Restructure:** Change the offer, channel, costs, team, or operating model.
- **Exit:** Sell, transfer, wind down, or discontinue under the applicable agreements and law.

Record the decision, evidence, financial impact, and approval.

## 3. Venture charter

Every pilot or venture should have a charter before material resources are committed.

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Venture ID and name | Unique internal identifier and working title |
| Problem and customer | Target user, need, and evidence |
| Offer | Product/service and value proposition |
| Stage | Discover, Validate, Build, Launch, Operate, Scale/Hold/Exit |
| Accountable owner | Person responsible for execution |
| Contributors | Roles and agreed scope |
| Budget | Approved cap, source, and release conditions |
| Commercial model | Pricing, channels, and expected revenue logic |
| Unit economics | Direct costs, margin assumptions, and sensitivities |
| Ownership and IP | Explicitly agreed rights and licenses |
| Risks | Key risks, mitigations, and owners |
| Success criteria | Measurable outcomes and review date |
| Stop conditions | Conditions that pause or terminate the pilot |

## 4. Portfolio intake and prioritization

Score opportunities using evidence and explicit assumptions. A suggested 1–5 scale:

- Customer pain and urgency — 20%.
- Evidence of willingness to pay — 20%.
- Access to customers and distribution — 15%.
- Gross-margin potential — 15%.
- Execution capability and time to pilot — 10%.
- Capital efficiency — 10%.
- Strategic learning or reusable assets — 5%.
- Risk and compliance profile — 5%.

A score is a prioritization aid, not a substitute for judgment. A serious legal, safety, integrity, or solvency concern can disqualify an opportunity regardless of score.

## 5. Portfolio decision rules

- Prefer small, reversible experiments before large commitments.
- Do not fund a new pilot by silently using another venture's restricted cash.
- Do not assume a venture's revenue belongs to the whole portfolio.
- Do not cross-guarantee debts or liabilities without explicit legal review and approval.
- Reassess underperforming ventures using predefined stop conditions.
- Preserve customer obligations and legal records when pausing or closing a venture.

## 6. Project finance and reporting

Each venture should maintain:
- Opening funding and source.
- Approved budget and revisions.
- Actual cash receipts and payments.
- Payables, receivables, refunds, and taxes.
- Assets, depreciation or impairment where applicable.
- Loans and repayment terms.
- Contributor compensation and reimbursements.
- Reserves and distributable-surplus calculations.

Suggested monthly dashboard:
- Revenue received.
- Gross profit and gross margin.
- Operating expenses.
- Net operating result.
- Cash balance and runway.
- Receivables and payables.
- Customer acquisition and retention.
- Delivery quality and outstanding risks.
- Budget variance and next decision.

## 7. Decision rights

Define authority at venture level before operations begin.

**Routine operations:** delegated to the accountable operator within the approved budget and scope.

**Reserved matters:** require the approval specified in the signed venture agreement. Examples include:
- Exceeding the approved budget.
- Taking loans or granting guarantees.
- Selling or pledging material assets.
- Issuing or transferring ownership.
- Admitting new capital providers.
- Materially changing business scope.
- Transferring or exclusively licensing core IP.
- Entering related-party transactions.
- Dissolving or selling the venture.

No approval matrix in this template overrides applicable law or a signed agreement.

## 8. Intellectual property and data

For each venture, record:
- Pre-existing IP and its owner.
- New work created and its agreed owner.
- Licenses, permitted use, and restrictions.
- Third-party software, content, and data licenses.
- Customer and personal data collected, purpose, access, retention, and deletion rules.
- Handover requirements if a contributor exits.

Do not assume that paying for work automatically transfers every IP right; document the intended arrangement and obtain legal review where needed.

## 9. Automation and technology controls

Automate repeatable, low-risk workflows first:
- Intake and opportunity tracking.
- Task routing and reminders.
- Budget and KPI reporting.
- Document versioning and approval logs.
- Customer support triage.
- Operational alerts.

Keep human approval for financial commitments, contracts, ownership changes, sensitive data access, and other reserved matters. Use least-privilege access, audit logs, backups, and documented recovery procedures.

## 10. Review cadence

- Weekly: active pilot execution, blockers, customer feedback, and cash exceptions.
- Monthly: venture performance, budget variance, risk, and portfolio review.
- Quarterly: strategic priorities, capital allocation, and continue/hold/exit decisions.
- Event-driven: immediate review after a material incident, legal change, major loss, or proposed ownership/financing change.

## 11. Required records

Maintain a controlled record set for each venture:
1. Venture charter.
2. Validation evidence and assumptions log.
3. Approved budget and change history.
4. Contributor and role register.
5. Ownership, contribution, and IP agreements.
6. Contracts, licenses, and compliance checklist.
7. Financial ledger and monthly dashboard.
8. Risk register and incident log.
9. Decision and approval log.
10. Exit or handover plan.

## 12. Operating standard

A venture is not considered ready for material scale merely because an idea is attractive or an MVP exists. It must demonstrate customer evidence, reliable delivery, understandable economics, accountable ownership, and controls appropriate to its risk.


## Commerce venture operating gate

Before a venture sells an offer, its charter must record:
- Legal seller/contracting party.
- Product owner and rights/license basis.
- Customer segment, offer, price, and currency.
- Channel and distribution permissions.
- Fulfillment owner, service levels, and capacity.
- Refunds, complaints, and warranty responsibility.
- Payment provider/merchant account.
- Unit economics and reconciliation method.
- Privacy/data purpose and retention.
- Launch, pause, and stop conditions.

A venture is not launch-ready merely because its product is listed. It must accept an order, fulfill the promise, support the customer, and reconcile the financial outcome.
