# MAELVYN — Master System

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Foundational system specification  
**Product:** MAELVYN — Shared Venture Studio  
**Operating model:** Active Venture Studio  
**Internal architecture:** Shared Economic Core

> Building Businesses. Creating Assets. Growing Together.  
> Participation by Choice. Ownership by Agreement.

## 1. System mandate

MAELVYN is a governed venture-building system for discovering opportunities, validating demand, forming independently accountable ventures, building and operating them, and managing a portfolio of productive businesses and assets.

It is not, by default, a pooled wallet, investment fund, holding company, legal partnership, public investment marketplace, or automatic merger of existing businesses.

The system must turn ideas into evidence-backed decisions and controlled execution. It must preserve the legal, financial, operational, and intellectual-property boundaries of every person and venture.

## 2. Foundational hierarchy

The following hierarchy governs all product, operational, and technical decisions:

1. Applicable law, executed agreements, and mandatory regulatory obligations.
2. Explicitly approved governance decisions and venture-specific charters.
3. This Master System and its non-negotiable safeguards.
4. Supporting policies, operating procedures, and product requirements.
5. Implementation details, automation, AI outputs, and convenience features.

A lower-level document, prompt, agent, workflow, or UI must never override a higher-level rule. Conflicts must be escalated for human review and documented resolution.

## 3. System architecture

### 3.1 Shared Economic Core

The coordination and control plane. It may manage:
- Strategy and portfolio priorities.
- Governance, decision rights, approvals, and decision records.
- Capital planning, budget controls, and financial oversight.
- Contribution, agreement, ownership, and IP registers.
- Risk, compliance, performance, and audit reporting.
- Shared operational standards and approved services.

The Core coordinates. It does not automatically own participant property, venture equity, revenue, or intellectual property.

### 3.2 Active Venture Studio

The venture execution lifecycle:

`Signal → Opportunity → Research → Validation → Charter → Build → Launch → Operate → Review → Scale / Hold / Restructure / Exit`

Each transition requires defined evidence and an accountable human decision owner. Research interest alone is not validation; a prototype alone is not a business; revenue alone is not proof of sustainable economics.

### 3.3 Portfolio layer

Every venture or productive asset has its own:
- Unique identity, purpose, scope, and lifecycle state.
- Responsible operator and decision rights.
- Charter, milestones, and evidence.
- Budget, transactions, liabilities, and reserves.
- Contributors, agreements, ownership, and IP rights.
- Risk register, compliance gates, and reporting cadence.
- Exit, transfer, continuity, and dispute provisions.

Consolidated portfolio reporting must not erase project-level separation.

## 4. Portfolio domains

1. **Digital Ventures & Assets:** software, SaaS, platforms, websites, digital products, content assets, and licenses.
2. **Business & Commerce Ventures:** products, brands, services, distribution, and commercial operations.
3. **Productive Assets:** equipment, machinery, tools, inventory, and assets used to generate operating income.
4. **Investment & Capital Ventures:** project investment and equity participation only after legal, regulatory, suitability, and risk review.

These are opportunity domains, not a mandate to launch or invest.

## 5. Eight operating engines

| Engine | System responsibility | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Governance | Decision rights, approvals, accountability, disputes | Decision record and authority |
| Capital | Budgets, funding plans, cash controls, allocation | Approved budget and source |
| Opportunity | Signals, research, customer problems, prioritization | Dated sources and validation |
| Venture Creation | Charter, MVP, launch, iteration | Milestones and customer evidence |
| Asset | Ownership, custody, use, maintenance, disposal | Rights and asset register |
| Revenue | Pricing, sales, collections, unit economics | Source-backed transactions |
| Automation | Repeatable workflows and integrations | Owner, logs, failure path |
| Growth | Distribution, partnerships, expansion, learning | Measured outcomes |

Automation supports accountable decisions; it does not replace reserved human approvals.

## 6. Non-negotiable rights and boundaries

- Participation is voluntary, informed, specific, and revocable according to applicable agreements.
- Silence, social-media views, informal interest, or non-response never constitute consent.
- No person is presumed to be a participant, investor, employee, partner, guarantor, or contributor.
- No person is required to contribute cash, labor, IP, equipment, borrow money, leave employment, or join every venture.
- Personal assets, accounts, funds, equipment, IP, and pre-existing ventures remain separate unless expressly transferred or licensed through a specific written agreement.
- Existing projects—including projects owned or operated outside MAELVYN—are not automatically included in the portfolio.
- No default 50:50 ownership or assumed profit share exists.
- A contribution is not automatically equity. Classify it as service, loan, license, sale, asset use, capital contribution, or another explicit arrangement.
- Ownership becomes effective only through the applicable executed agreement and required legal steps.
- No public claim may imply a partnership, endorsement, investment offer, guarantee, or participant commitment without authorization.

## 7. Governance model

Every material decision must identify:
- Decision owner and authority source.
- Affected venture, assets, people, and agreements.
- Options considered and supporting evidence.
- Financial and non-financial impact.
- Conflicts of interest.
- Required approvers and approval threshold.
- Decision, rationale, timestamp, conditions, and review date.

Reserved matters include, at minimum, new debt or guarantees, material asset sales or transfers, ownership changes, admission of investors, distributions, material changes to participant obligations, and legal-entity formation. The affected rights-holders must explicitly approve as required by the applicable agreement and law.

AI may summarize, research, draft, classify, and recommend. AI must not impersonate a participant, infer consent, cast a binding vote, self-approve a reserved matter, or execute an unapproved payment or commitment.

## 8. Financial constitution

Maintain venture-level records and distinguish:
- Cash received.
- Recognized revenue.
- Refunds, payment fees, and direct costs.
- Operating expenses and supplier/payroll obligations.
- Taxes and statutory liabilities.
- Debt and other payables.
- Restricted and operating reserves.
- Genuine distributable surplus.

Illustrative cash waterfall:

1. Cash revenue actually received.
2. Refunds, payment fees, and direct transaction costs.
3. Operating costs, suppliers, payroll, and applicable taxes.
4. Debt service and other due liabilities.
5. Agreed operating and contingency reserves.
6. Genuine distributable surplus.
7. Reinvestment, reserve allocation, or distribution under executed agreements.

Revenue is not profit. Accounting profit is not necessarily available cash. A budget approval is not payment authorization. No distribution or return is guaranteed.

Financial invariants:
- Record amounts in integer minor units and explicit ISO currency codes.
- Use double-entry accounting for posted journals.
- Total debits must equal total credits per currency and journal.
- Posted entries are immutable; corrections use reversals.
- Reconcile records to evidence and source accounts.
- Separate proposal, approval, payment execution, and ledger posting.
- Never fabricate transactions, balances, valuations, or returns.

## 9. Venture lifecycle gates

| Gate | Minimum requirements before transition |
|---|---|
| Signal → Opportunity | Source, date, relevance, initial problem hypothesis |
| Opportunity → Research | Defined customer, market, assumptions, research plan |
| Research → Validation | Evidence log, alternatives, risks, test design |
| Validation → Charter | Customer signal, feasibility, economics hypothesis, accountable operator |
| Charter → Build | Approved scope, budget, contributors, IP terms, risks, success metric |
| Build → Launch | QA, delivery process, pricing, support, privacy/security checks |
| Launch → Operate | Real usage/sales evidence, reconciled records, operating owner |
| Operate → Scale/Hold/etc. | Review against unit economics, cash, retention, risks, capacity |

A failed gate means revise, pause, or stop—not bypass the gate.

## 10. North Star

**North Star Metric: Evidence-Backed Active Ventures (EBAV).**

A venture counts only when all conditions are true:
1. It is in Build, Launch, or Operate.
2. It has an accountable human operator.
3. Its charter and current budget are approved.
4. It has recent, reviewable evidence appropriate to its stage.
5. It has no unresolved critical governance, legal, financial, privacy, or security gate.

Do not inflate EBAV with ideas, dormant projects, unapproved pilots, vanity metrics, or duplicate records.

Supporting measures include validated customer problems, time to evidence, launch quality, revenue quality, gross margin, cash conversion, retention, asset utilization, operating reliability, compliance completion, and portfolio risk.

## 11. System product boundaries

The application may provide:
- Public information pages with approved claims.
- Authenticated workspace and membership.
- Opportunity pipeline and evidence log.
- Venture registry, charter, lifecycle, and review.
- Contribution and rights records.
- Agreement metadata and controlled private document access.
- Approval inbox and decision history.
- Budget, ledger, assets, and reporting.
- Audit and security administration.

The initial release must not provide:
- Public investment solicitation or an investment marketplace.
- Custody of participant money or pooled wallet.
- Automatic legally binding profit-sharing or dividend entitlement.
- Automatic import or transfer of existing businesses and assets.
- Autonomous approval or execution of loans, investments, ownership changes, or payments.
- Unrestricted public signup or access to confidential portfolio data.

## 12. Technical invariants

- Server-side authorization on every private read and write.
- Deny by default; least privilege; venture-scoped access.
- Client-supplied role or workspace ID is never proof of authority.
- Strong separation of development, staging, and production.
- No production data or secrets in preview/development.
- Private document storage and short-lived authorized access.
- Audit security-sensitive and financially material actions.
- Never log credentials, secrets, full payment credentials, or unnecessary sensitive data.
- Use reviewed, versioned migrations; do not manually mutate production schema.
- Idempotency and explicit failure handling for external side effects.
- Backups and restore tests before production launch.
- Provider-neutral adapters where provider lock-in is not yet justified.

## 13. Legal, privacy, and launch gates

Before accepting pooled funds, issuing ownership, taking investment, borrowing, hiring, transferring assets, or forming a legal entity:
1. Identify actual parties, roles, rights, and obligations.
2. Select a jurisdiction-appropriate legal and tax structure.
3. Obtain qualified legal/accounting review.
4. Prepare and execute required agreements.
5. Establish compliant financial, privacy, and operational controls.

Before real participant or financial data is stored, complete privacy/terms review, access and audit tests, restore demonstration, production isolation, incident/offboarding procedures, and responsible-operator assignment.

This document is an operating and product specification, not legal, tax, investment, or accounting advice.

## 14. Change control

Every material change must record:
- Change ID, author, date, and reason.
- Affected principle, domain, interface, schema, or process.
- Impact on rights, money, security, privacy, and existing records.
- Migration/backward-compatibility plan.
- Required human approvals.
- Tests and rollback plan.
- Updated version and decision record.

When uncertain, stop the affected action, preserve evidence, and request clarification from the authorized human decision owner.

## 15. Master rule

**MAELVYN creates value through evidence-backed venture building while preserving individual consent, separate property, explicit agreements, transparent finances, and accountable human decisions.**
