# MAELVYN — Master Blueprint & Operating Constitution

**Document status:** Foundational proposal  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Model:** Active Venture Studio  
**Working identity:** MAELVYN — Shared Venture Studio

## 1. Purpose

MAELVYN is designed as a long-term framework for discovering, validating, creating, operating, and growing businesses and productive assets.

The ambition is to build real operating value through disciplined venture creation—not simply to combine money or hold investments passively.

This document defines the proposed principles and system boundaries. It is not a partnership agreement, investment contract, company constitution, or legal instrument.

## 2. Vision and mission

### Vision
Build a durable portfolio of independently accountable ventures and productive assets that can create sustainable revenue, capabilities, and long-term value.

### Mission
- Find meaningful market opportunities.
- Test demand before committing substantial resources.
- Build practical products, services, and operating businesses.
- Establish reliable operations and transparent financial records.
- Reinvest responsibly and grow the portfolio based on evidence.
- Protect the autonomy and rights of every participant.

### Tagline
**Building Businesses. Creating Assets. Growing Together.**

## 3. The MAELVYN architecture

### 3.1 Shared Economic Core

The coordination and governance layer. Its responsibilities may include:
- Strategic direction and portfolio priorities.
- Governance, decision rights, and approvals.
- Capital allocation and financial oversight.
- Contribution, ownership, and intellectual-property registers.
- Risk, compliance, and performance monitoring.
- Consolidated reporting without erasing project-level accountability.

The Core coordinates; it does not automatically own every participant's property or every venture.

### 3.2 Active Venture Studio

The execution layer responsible for:
- Opportunity research.
- Customer and market validation.
- Business-model design.
- MVP and initial product development.
- Launch and customer acquisition.
- Operations, measurement, and improvement.
- Scale, hold, restructure, partnership, or exit decisions.

### 3.3 Portfolio layer

Each venture is treated as a separately scoped project with its own:
- Business purpose and operating plan.
- Budget, cash records, and financial statements.
- Contributors and agreed responsibilities.
- Ownership and intellectual-property records.
- Contracts, liabilities, and risk register.
- Approval thresholds and reporting cadence.
- Exit, transfer, and dispute provisions.

Shared coordination does not mean automatic pooling of assets, liabilities, or revenue.

## 4. Portfolio categories

1. **Digital Ventures & Assets** — software, SaaS, websites, platforms, digital products, content assets, and licenses.
2. **Business & Commerce Ventures** — brands, products, distribution, services, reseller models, and commercial partnerships.
3. **Productive Assets** — equipment, machinery, tools, productive inventory, and assets used to generate operating income.
4. **Investment & Capital Ventures** — carefully evaluated equity participation, project investments, and allocation of genuine surplus, subject to applicable law and risk controls.

A category is a strategic area, not an instruction to invest or launch immediately.

## 5. Eight operating engines

| Engine | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Governance Engine | Decisions, approvals, accountability, and dispute handling |
| Capital Engine | Budgets, funding plans, cash controls, and allocation |
| Opportunity Engine | Research, market signals, and opportunity pipeline |
| Venture Creation Engine | Validation, MVP, launch, and venture development |
| Asset Engine | Asset register, utilization, maintenance, and ownership |
| Revenue Engine | Pricing, sales, collections, and customer economics |
| Automation Engine | Repeatable workflows, integrations, and operational controls |
| Growth Engine | Distribution, partnerships, expansion, and portfolio learning |

Automation supports accountable human decisions; it does not replace required approvals.

## 6. Participation and relationship boundaries

Participation is always voluntary and may take different forms, if freely agreed:
- Co-founder or venture partner.
- Strategic or operating partner.
- Project-specific investor.
- Advisor, specialist, or reviewer.
- Independent collaborator.
- No participation.

No person's interest, consent, commitment, or availability should be inferred from silence, social-media activity, or informal conversation.

No person is obligated to:
- Contribute money, time, skills, assets, or intellectual property.
- Borrow money or assume another person's liabilities.
- leave employment or change personal circumstances.
- Join every venture or accept every proposal.

A concept proposal does not create a partnership or financial obligation.

## 7. Property, contribution, and ownership safeguards

### 7.1 Separate property by default

Each person's pre-existing money, bank accounts, equipment, intellectual property, business interests, and ventures remain their own property unless a separate written agreement expressly provides otherwise.

Existing businesses or projects are not automatically contributed, transferred, licensed, or placed under MAELVYN.

### 7.2 Contributions

Potential contributions may include cash, labor, expertise, technology, intellectual property, networks, equipment, or other assets.

Before a contribution is accepted, document:
- What is being contributed.
- Who owns it before contribution.
- Its agreed valuation or valuation method, if relevant.
- Whether it is a loan, service, license, sale, capital contribution, or other arrangement.
- The rights and obligations it creates.
- How it may be returned, transferred, or terminated.

### 7.3 Ownership

There is no default 50:50 ownership rule. Ownership must be expressly agreed for each legal entity or venture, with clear treatment of cash and non-cash contributions, vesting where relevant, decision rights, dilution, transfers, and exits.

## 8. Governance and approval principles

Before a venture becomes operational, define:
- Responsible operator and decision owner.
- Routine decisions that can be made independently.
- Spending limits and approval thresholds.
- Matters requiring unanimous or specified participant approval.
- Reporting schedule and access to records.
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure.
- Deadlock and dispute-resolution process.

At minimum, new debt, guarantees, material asset sales, admission of investors, changes in ownership, and material changes to participant obligations require explicit documented approval from the affected rights-holders.

## 9. Financial constitution

Use a transparent project-level cash and accounting model.

**Illustrative waterfall:**

1. Cash revenue actually received.
2. Refunds, payment fees, and direct transaction costs.
3. Operating costs, supplier obligations, payroll, and applicable taxes.
4. Debt service and other due liabilities.
5. Agreed operating and contingency reserves.
6. Genuine distributable surplus.
7. Allocation of surplus to reinvestment, reserves, or distribution under the venture's written agreement.

Revenue is not profit. Accounting profit is not necessarily available cash. No distribution should be promised before liabilities, taxes, reserves, and applicable agreements are considered.

## 10. Risk, compliance, and integrity

Every venture should maintain a proportionate risk register covering:
- Market and customer demand.
- Cash flow and capital exposure.
- Operational and supplier dependency.
- Legal, tax, licensing, and regulatory requirements.
- Privacy, security, and data handling.
- Intellectual property and third-party licenses.
- Reputation, conflicts of interest, and continuity.

Do not present unverified claims, guaranteed returns, or unapproved partnerships as facts.

## 11. Exit, separation, and continuity

Each venture agreement should address:
- Voluntary withdrawal.
- Transfer restrictions and valuation.
- Treatment of outstanding contributions and liabilities.
- Buyout or sale mechanisms, if appropriate.
- Access to records and handover.
- Ownership and continued use of intellectual property.
- Confidentiality and lawful data retention.
- Deadlock, dissolution, and dispute resolution.

A person's departure from one venture should not automatically transfer their rights in unrelated ventures.

## 12. Legal implementation gate

Before accepting pooled funds, issuing ownership, taking investment, hiring, borrowing, transferring assets, or forming a legal entity:
1. Identify the actual parties and their roles.
2. Select an appropriate legal and tax structure.
3. Obtain jurisdiction-specific legal and accounting review.
4. Prepare and sign the required agreements.
5. Establish compliant financial, recordkeeping, and approval processes.

This blueprint is an operating-design document, not legal advice. The applicable requirements depend on the actual activities, parties, and jurisdiction.

## 13. Success measures

Measure progress through evidence:
- Validated customer problems and willingness to pay.
- MVPs launched and customer retention.
- Revenue quality, gross margin, and cash conversion.
- Operating reliability and customer satisfaction.
- Productive asset utilization and return.
- Risk exposure and compliance completion.
- Quality of records and timeliness of reporting.
- Sustainable value created—not vanity metrics alone.

## 14. Foundational rule

**MAELVYN may coordinate shared ambition, but it must never erase individual consent, ownership, accountability, or financial boundaries.**


## Commerce ecosystem extension (2026-11-18)

The operating model is extended by a Hybrid Commerce Ecosystem: Central Marketplace + Independent Venture Storefronts + Partner & Distribution Network. Scope includes digital products, software/SaaS, AI products, services, physical goods, subscriptions, licensing/IP, B2B offers, affiliate, reseller, and distribution activities, subject to legal/provider eligibility.

Commerce operates as shared infrastructure for separately accountable ventures. It does not make MAELVYN the automatic legal seller or owner of every product. Seller identity, ownership/licensing, customer data, liabilities, fulfillment, and financial records must be explicit per offer. See `docs/COMMERCE-OS-MASTER-ARCHITECTURE.md`.

The proposed initial legal operating arrangement is described in `docs/LEGAL-ENTITY-AND-OPERATING-MODEL.md`; it is not a legal conclusion and requires verification of the existing entity's status, KBLI, permits, tax, and contracts.
