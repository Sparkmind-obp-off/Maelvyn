# MAELVYN — Business Operating Model

**Status:** Operating charter / design baseline  
**Last reviewed:** 21 November 2026  
**Business identity:** Agentic Shared Venture Studio  
**Tagline:** Building Businesses. Creating Assets. Growing Together.  
**Participation principle:** Participation by Choice. Ownership by Agreement.

## 1. What MAELVYN is

MAELVYN is a business-building and venture-operating studio. It discovers problems, validates demand, creates and operates ventures, commercializes products and productive assets, and shares selected capabilities across a deliberately governed portfolio.

MAELVYN is not merely a software project, AI chatbot wrapper, freelancer directory, public investment marketplace, or passive holding company.

**MAELVYN OS is the operating infrastructure. The business portfolio and its commercial outcomes are the objective.**

The studio must be judged by customers served, products shipped, cash generated, margins, venture survival, and assets created—not by the number of agents, workflows, dashboards, integrations, or documents.

## 2. The two-engine model

MAELVYN operates two related but financially and governably distinct engines.

### A. Venture Studio

The Studio is responsible for:
- opportunity thesis and problem discovery;
- customer research and validation;
- venture selection, formation, and charter;
- accountable venture operator assignment;
- portfolio capital/resource allocation;
- strategic review, restructuring, hold, and closure decisions;
- ownership, contribution, IP, and participation agreements.

The Studio does not assume every idea deserves a company. Ideas must pass evidence-based gates. Failed ideas are recorded with their evidence and learning, then stopped or parked.

### B. Product & Service Factory

The Factory converts reusable capabilities into sellable, quality-controlled outcomes:
- research and intelligence products;
- software, micro-SaaS, and workflow automation;
- creative and digital production;
- customer operations and managed services;
- commerce operations and digital asset packages.

The Factory can sell to external customers at transparent market prices. It can also deliver work for a portfolio venture under an explicit internal service order and cost allocation.

The Factory must not invoice a venture for work already funded as a capital contribution. Avoid double charging and undisclosed cross-subsidy.

### Why both engines are necessary

The Factory provides a practical path to early operating revenue and reusable production assets. The Studio uses evidence and disciplined allocation to build a portfolio over time. Factory revenue is not proof that a venture thesis has been validated; venture equity upside is not a substitute for Factory cash flow.

## 3. Revenue architecture

MAELVYN may earn revenue through:
1. direct sales of Factory products and packaged deliverables;
2. productized services and managed operations;
3. software subscriptions, usage plans, and licenses;
4. distribution and partner commissions where contractually permitted;
5. IP licenses and royalties where rights are documented;
6. venture-level revenue and, only under separate written agreements, venture participation or exit proceeds.

No revenue stream may be described as active until there is a real offer, a lawful seller, a price, an order or contract, a fulfillment record, and a reconciled payment record.

MAELVYN must not solicit public investment, promise returns, or offer unlicensed investment products through the platform.

## 4. Commercial launch discipline

The first commercial wedge is a **hypothesis**, not a proven market fact. Select one narrow buyer, one painful job, one measurable deliverable, and one price. Validate it with real prospective customers before building a broad platform.

Recommended sequence:
1. Interview at least 15–30 target buyers in one defined segment.
2. Record the current workaround, cost of the problem, decision-maker, urgency, and existing budget.
3. Present a scoped offer and ask for a paid pilot or a written commitment.
4. Require at least three independent buying signals before committing substantial build capacity.
5. Deliver the pilot manually or with supervised automation.
6. Record accepted outcome, delivery time, direct cost, provider cost, rework, refund/dispute, and contribution margin.
7. Standardize only the parts that repeat.
8. Decide to proceed, revise, hold, or stop.

A signed LOI is a signal, not revenue. A verbal compliment is not validation. A free trial is not a paid customer.

## 5. Venture lifecycle and stage gates

| Gate | Decision | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| G0 — Discover | Is the problem worth investigating? | Defined customer segment, problem statement, source trail, named owner |
| G1 — Validate | Will a buyer commit? | Customer interviews, buying signals, pilot scope, pricing hypothesis, unit economics |
| G2 — Build | Can we deliver reliably? | Approved charter, budget, MVP/delivery spec, QA criteria, rights review |
| G3 — Launch | Can we sell and fulfill safely? | Seller identity, terms, privacy notice, payment/refund procedure, support path, delivery test |
| G4 — Operate | Is the business healthy? | Paid orders, acceptance, margin, cash reconciliation, customer feedback, incident status |
| G5 — Scale / Spinout | Can it operate with greater independence? | Repeatable demand, accountable CEO/operator, separate books, clean IP and contracts, financing plan |

Each gate ends with a recorded decision: advance, rework, hold, or reject. Every rejection includes the evidence and the condition that would justify reopening the idea.

## 6. Studio shared services

The shared layer should remain small and reusable:
- governance, risk, legal-template coordination, and rights records;
- finance, bookkeeping, budget control, and unit economics;
- customer research and validation;
- brand, design, product, and software engineering;
- agent orchestration, provider adapters, QA, and observability;
- customer support and distribution operations;
- reusable playbooks, templates, and technical components.

Shared services must have explicit service scopes, owners, cost attribution, and data boundaries.

## 7. North Star and operating scorecard

**North Star: Evidence-Backed Active Ventures (EBAV).**

A venture counts only when it has:
- an approved charter and accountable operator;
- an approved budget or documented zero-cash operating plan;
- recent customer/market evidence;
- a current operating review;
- no unresolved critical governance, legal, privacy, or security gate.

Core business metrics:
- qualified customer conversations and conversion;
- paid orders and successful fulfillment;
- recognized/cash-collected revenue by seller, venture, offer, and channel;
- direct cost and contribution margin;
- acquisition cost and payback where measurable;
- acceptance, revision, refund, dispute, and repeat purchase rates;
- delivery time and capacity utilization;
- cash runway and budget variance.

Core system metrics:
- accepted task completion rate;
- human rework per accepted outcome;
- provider cost per accepted outcome;
- approval integrity and audit coverage;
- retry/idempotency and recovery success;
- cross-venture data isolation violations (target: zero).

## 8. What MAELVYN will not claim

MAELVYN will not claim:
- that a venture is validated without customer evidence;
- that revenue exists before payment/order reconciliation;
- that an agent is autonomous without tested permissions and bounded execution;
- that a marketplace integration can publish or transact unless seller-side capability is verified;
- that MAELVYN owns an asset or IP without a documented legal basis;
- that a provider's free tier is unlimited or commercially usable without checking its terms;
- that a production system is ready while required secrets, storage, security, or operational controls remain unverified.

## 9. Legal and accounting separation

This operating model is a business design, not legal or tax advice. Before issuing offers, collecting customer data, employing contractors, assigning IP, sharing venture ownership, or operating regulated activities, confirm the correct legal seller, KBLI/licensing, tax treatment, consumer terms, privacy obligations, and written agreements with qualified Indonesian professionals.

The MAELVYN workspace is not itself a legal entity. Each venture's legal seller, asset owner, IP holder, bank/payment account, and tax responsibility must be recorded explicitly.
